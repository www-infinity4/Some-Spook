## Indium Metal — Properties and Uses

### What Is Indium?

Indium is a very soft, silvery-white post-transition metal (element symbol **In**, atomic number 49). It is best known in popular science demonstrations for being shaped into a stick that can be bent and deformed by hand. When bent, its crystals slide against each other and produce an audible crackling sound — a phenomenon known as **"twinning cry"** (or "twin cry").

### Key Properties

- **Appearance:** Lustrous, silvery-white metal
- **Hardness:** Extremely soft — softer than lead and even softer than most other metals; can be scratched with a fingernail
- **Malleability:** Highly malleable; can be flattened into thin sheets or shaped into sticks by hand
- **Twinning cry:** When an indium bar is bent, the internal crystal lattice undergoes mechanical twinning, producing a characteristic crackling or crunching sound

### Radioactivity

Indium has two naturally occurring isotopes:

| Isotope | Abundance | Radioactive? |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| Indium-113 | ~4.3% | No (stable) |
| Indium-115 | ~95.7% | Yes (beta emitter) |

Although **Indium-115** is technically radioactive, its half-life is approximately **4.41 × 10¹⁴ years** (over 400 trillion years) — many orders of magnitude longer than the age of the universe. This makes its radioactivity entirely negligible and not considered a health hazard for ordinary handling or use.

### Common Uses

- **Electronics:** Indium tin oxide (ITO) is widely used as a transparent conducting coating in LCD displays, touchscreens, and solar cells
- **Semiconductors:** Used in compound semiconductors such as indium phosphide (InP) and indium gallium arsenide (InGaAs) for high-speed electronics and photonics
- **Solders and alloys:** Added to low-melting-point alloys and solders for specialty applications
- **Bearings:** Used in thin-film coatings to reduce friction and corrosion in bearings
- **Science demonstrations:** Its softness and twinning cry make it a popular material for hands-on physics and materials-science demonstrations

### Safety

While indium is considered to have **low acute toxicity** in metallic form, it is generally not recommended to ingest or chew metal objects made from it. Long-term health data on indium exposure is limited, and chewing any hard metal can damage teeth. Indium compounds (particularly indium oxide dust) can pose inhalation risks in occupational settings and should be handled with appropriate precautions.
